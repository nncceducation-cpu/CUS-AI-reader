CUS AI Reader · v0.6.0 · research use only

The scoring path between the model and the grade, rebuilt

Ten defects sat between the model output and the consensus grade. Two meant the tool could never produce a grade at all, and one meant a real ultrasound sweep would not load. This release fixes the path, adds calibration and anatomic constraints, pins every clinical rule to the 2021 Canadian consensus paper, and lets the tool learn from being corrected.

What was broken

Found by reading the scoring path against the consensus algorithm it claims to implement.

1

A single bright frame could carry a finding

Frame-to-study pooling took the mean of the three highest frame probabilities. On a 400-frame sweep that is close to a maximum. Three noisy frames declared any finding present, and speckle, the choroid plexus edge, and the caudothalamic notch all produce isolated bright frames.

Fixed. A finding must now persist. The score is the more conservative of the k-th largest probability and the highest level sustained across a run of consecutive frames, and where the anatomy allows it, a second plane must confirm.

2

Step 2 of the algorithm was unanswerable

confined_to_germinal_matrix had no label anywhere in the manifest contract, so it resolved to unknown on every study. Step 2 could never be answered, which pinned every AI grade at Indeterminate GMH-IVH grade regardless of the images.

Fixed. Step 2 is derived, exactly as the algorithm derives it: hemorrhage present with no intraventricular blood is confined to the germinal matrix. Grade I and grade III now resolve.

3

The tool abstained on every study

Abstention was global. Any one uncertain feature set it, and so did the default absence of a serial study. Since that default is off, every study abstained on every domain, including the domains the model had answered confidently.

Fixed. Abstention is scoped per domain. A clean coronal sweep with no mastoid window now reports its hemorrhage grades and withholds cerebellar hemorrhage, with the reason attached. That is what a reader does.

4

Alias matching biased every field toward yes

Evidence fields were resolved by taking whichever candidate label scored highest. hemorrhage_present listed intraventricular_blood as an alias, so intraventricular scores could raise hemorrhage presence but never lower it. One-sided error, on every field with more than one alias.

Fixed. Alias lookup is gone. Fields a network can emit are read directly; fields that are logical consequences are derived.

5

PVHI was fed back in as its own input

A label literally named pvhi was accepted as the input for periventricular echogenicity. PVHI is the conclusion Step 4 reaches, so accepting it as an input short-circuited the rule it was meant to feed.

Fixed. Step 4 reads focal periventricular echogenicity only.

6

Raw probabilities met fixed thresholds

Multi-task heads are over-confident. A threshold of 0.50 applied to uncalibrated output is not the operating point the number implies, and nothing in the pipeline corrected for it.

Fixed. Platt, isotonic, or temperature calibration carried in the manifest, applied per frame before aggregation. scripts/fit_operating_point.py fits it from your labelled studies and picks thresholds that weight a missed bleed above a second read.

7

Impossible outputs were graded as though possible

Nothing linked related heads. The model could report P(AHW > 10 mm) = 0.80 alongside P(AHW > 6 mm) = 0.40. Every ventricle wider than 10 mm is also wider than 6 mm, so that pair cannot happen, and the rules graded the contradiction instead of flagging it.

Fixed. Nested thresholds are made monotone, containment hierarchies enforced, exclusive grade families renormalised. Every repair is logged, because frequent repair is itself a finding about the model.

8

Cerebellar findings were scored from coronal frames

Every label was scored from every accepted frame. Cerebellar hemorrhage probabilities were pooled from coronal frontal-horn frames, where the cerebellum is not in view.

Fixed. Each label family is restricted to the planes that can carry it. Cerebellar labels score from the posterior fossa only; AHW and VI from coronal only.

Calibration, separately

On a synthetic over-confident detector, Platt scaling moved log loss from 0.328 to 0.096 and the Brier score from 0.097 to 0.018, and removed the false positives that a naive 0.50 threshold produced on the same scores.

Clinical rules corrected

Read back against Front Pediatr 2021;9:618236 and the HUSDiagnostic decision tree. Each now has a named test.

RuleWasNow
Echodensity definition Any echogenicity counted Must exceed choroid plexus brightness. Peritrigonal blushes and symmetric frontal echodensities read as physiologic.
Step 4, negative at Step 1 "PVHI requires ipsilateral GMH-IVH" Reported as significant ischemic injury, which is what the paper says to report.
Bilateral Step 4 Not implemented Anchored to the largest GMH-IVH. Bilateral echogenicity with only small germinal matrix hemorrhage favours ischemic injury over bilateral PVHI.
Step 1 scope Lateral ventricle only Includes third and fourth ventricular blood.
PHVD timing Graded at any age Graded after day 7. Acute distension in the first week is grade III, not PHVD, which is a separation the consensus makes deliberately.
Severe injury definition Large CBH only Moderate to large CBH, per the paper.
Hemorrhage off the axis Fell through ungraded Reported explicitly, with a prompt to consider cerebellar, subdural, or parenchymal sources.

Two more things shipped after the scoring rebuild

Both found by testing against the real pilot clips rather than by reasoning about the code.

9

Real sweeps would not load

Every decoded frame was held as full-size RGB. The pilot sweeps are 1024×1024 and run from 112 to 2011 frames, so one clip costs between 0.35 and 5.6 GB and a coronal plus sagittal pair is 2 to 5 GB before the app caches a second copy. Across all 43 clips that is 88 GB. It killed the test machine outright on the first real sweep.

Fixed. Frames are stored grayscale with the longest side bounded to 512, which is the form the model and the quality metrics already consumed. Measured on a real 530-frame sweep: 1.67 GB becomes 139 MB, decoded in about a second, every frame still processed.

10

The tool could not learn from being corrected

Every correction a reader made was discarded. The shipped thresholds stayed shipped.

Added. The AI proposes a grade, the reader corrects it, and the decision thresholds and calibration are refitted from that site's own reads. Against a deliberately miscalibrated detector, scored on 80 studies never used for fitting, agreement rose from 41% to 96% after 60 corrections.

What stops the learning loop making things worse

The obvious implementation of "correct it and it improves" degrades accuracy while its own numbers improve.

Caught in testing. The first working version of the learner promoted new parameters on pure random noise in every single trial. Given signal-free scores it found the degenerate rule that calls every study positive, which scores well when misses are weighted four times higher than false alarms. It would have looked like learning and pushed the thresholds to extremes.

RiskWhat prevents it
Fitting and scoring on the same corrections Leave-one-infant-out cross-validation. Studies from one infant are not independent, so whole infants are held out. Every number shown is out of fold.
Learning a degenerate rule from noise A candidate must beat the incumbent, beat the best constant predictor, clear an out-of-fold AUC floor, and survive a permutation test against shuffled labels. Noise now promotes in about 1 run in 60.
Trading away sensitivity for specificity An absolute floor the clinical lead sets, default 0.95, checked out of fold on at least 12 held-out positives. The 95% lower bound is displayed next to it, always.
Learning the reader's anchoring to the AI Only grades recorded blind to the AI are eligible for fitting. Unblinded corrections are kept for audit and excluded from every fit.
Silent or unreviewable change Append-only correction log, versioned parameters with rollback, and the shipped manifest is never rewritten.

Thresholds unlock at 8 corrections in the smaller class across at least 3 infants; calibration at 20. Network weights are never touched, because they need thousands of examples. What the log gives you instead is an export button: every correction is a labelled row, and a few hundred of them is what makes a model trainable on Canadian consensus targets.

What comes next

This release fixes how model output becomes a grade. It does not train a model, and the gap between the two is the whole remaining problem. The order that makes sense:

  1. Adjudicate the pilot registry to a second reader. Fifteen single-reader examinations across eight infants is enough for workflow, not for fitting anything.
  2. Collect labelled per-frame scores and run fit_operating_point.py to replace the placeholder thresholds with fitted ones.
  3. Keep all examinations from one infant inside one partition, and hold an entire site out of training, tuning, and threshold selection.
  4. Lock the primary endpoint before analysis. Per-infant sensitivity for severe preterm brain injury, with centre-clustered resampling, is the endpoint the validation protocol already recommends.
  5. Only then set "validated": true.
